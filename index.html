What Changed
A Room, Not A Page

Deep espresso and warm ivory — it feels like walking into a recital hall instead of a school gym. Calm, uncluttered, and expensive-looking without shouting.

Real Brass, Not Gold Paint

An aged brass tone on the buttons, the thin dividing lines, and the piano keys across the middle. It reads like a real metal finish, not a cheap gold gradient.

Lettering With A Voice

An elegant italic for the headline and a clean, plain typeface for everything else — so your eye knows instantly what matters most on the page.

A "book a lesson" button before you scroll, and again everywhere you stop. Proof they're established sits right up top, where a nervous first-timer will see it.

Churches & Ministries

A church website is
a different job.

Most of the people who visit a church website are not members. They are someone who moved here in March, or someone having the worst month of their life at 11pm on a Tuesday. They came to answer three questions: when do you meet, where do I park, and what happens when I walk in.

Most church sites bury all three under an events calendar nobody updated since spring. We build the other kind.

Church website design

Service times, first thing

Times, address and parking above the fold on a phone — not three taps deep behind a hero video.

An "I'm new here" path

Giving that works on a phone

Connected to whatever platform you already use. Two taps from the home page, no app to install.

Sermons people can find

An archive that is searchable and embeddable — plus the publishing pipeline that keeps it fed.
