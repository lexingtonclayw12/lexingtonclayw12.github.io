Why it matters
here specifically.

There is a specific kind of business we end up helping most often here: established, well-reviewed, known by everyone who already knows them — and completely invisible to anyone who does not. Twenty years of reputation, and a site built in 2011 that a phone renders sideways.

That gap costs more in Rock Hill than people think, because Old Town and Knowledge Park keep bringing in people who have no history here. Winthrop turns over thousands of people who need a dentist, a mechanic, a church and a haircut and have nobody to ask.

Questions

Questions from
Rock Hill owners.

We already have a website. Is a rebuild worth it?

It is if the site is slow, unreadable on a phone, or does not say which town you are in — those three account for most of the ground small sites lose. The free evaluation will tell you which of them apply to yours, and if the honest answer is that your current site is fine, we will say so.

Will we lose our Google rankings if you rebuild it?

Not if it is done properly. We keep the pages that are already earning traffic, point the old addresses at the new ones with permanent redirects, and keep your business name, address and phone identical everywhere. Rankings usually recover within a couple of weeks and often improve, because the new site is faster.

Do you work with churches and nonprofits in Rock Hill?

Regularly, and it is a different build. Service times, giving, sermons and events matter more than a product catalogue, and the decision usually goes to a board rather than one person. There is a page for that — see church website design.
